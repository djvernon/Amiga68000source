I recently managed to recover the source for CC from some 22 year old floppies.
Sadly, while I have everything to rebuild the PC version, the disk routines and a few others bits are missing from the 68000 versions.
Still, the PC was better, other than the sound.
For more info on the CC remake, see the "Carrier Command" facebook page.

Regards web page, I'm typing up "The Story of Carrier Command" and might put it up somewhere.
Believe it or not, Tank Duel, Star Strike, and Star Strike 2 were coded on the Spectrum by typing Z80 assembler into REM statements.
Bombs? I don't recall what we did if an exception went off, but the final stages on the ST version were tense beyond belief.
A few bug fixes did get slipstreamed, but I don't recall many crashing bugs.

We signed the contract on the day after my 24th birthday in 1987. We then had 4.5 months to deliver on ST, one more month for Mac and Amiga, another month for PC,
and yet another for three Z80 versions. We must have been mad!
The ST version took just over a year, the Amiga came out in July 1988, and the PC version in July 1989.
The ST version was about 50,000 lines of 68000 assembler and the PC about 76,000 lines of 8086 assembler. We had no debug tools other than coffee.

Yup, just two of us on the 16-bit versions. We did the ST and Amiga versions first, and getting these out took the pressure off us.
This meant we could make improvements to the AI etc. for the PC version, but TBH there are little tweaks all over the place.
I'm currently writing a walk-through of the original PC code and may release this with the source some time.
However, it's about 50,000 line of 8086 assembler and it's not pretty!




Emulator notes
--------------
MAME m68000 generates all instruction handlers
These do have opcode in function names, but still not obvious e.g. 'm68k_op_add_16_er_ai'
They use shifting instead of byte/word/long union

WinUAE generates all instruction handlers
